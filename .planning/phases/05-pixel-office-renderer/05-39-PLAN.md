<objective>
Close G-05-1a: at stream resolution the waiting/hourglass glyph reads as a blue bowtie rather than
an hourglass, and the user must fall back on its colour to identify the state.

Root cause, found by reading the asset rather than guessing: the glyph's SILHOUETTE is already a
correct hourglass — four existing tests prove full-width 3-row caps, strictly triangular bulbs, a
single-pixel waist and sand in the lower bulb. What fails is the FILL. The top cap's interior is
painted in the same `#4361ee` as the sand, so the "empty upper bulb / full lower bulb" signal that
makes an hourglass legible is swamped: at a glance the whole thing is one blue mass with a pinch in
the middle, and only the blue tells you what it is. That is exactly what OFFICE-03 and D-03 forbid.

The fix is a repaint, not a redraw. The opaque mask does not change by a single cell, so every
existing silhouette, distinctness and placement guard stays green.

Purpose: make the glyph identifiable in grayscale.
Output: a repainted `bubble-waiting.json` and a grayscale band test that goes red on a revert.
</objective>

  <action>
Edit `packages/pixel-office/src/sprites/bubble-waiting.json`. Two changes, both fill-only — do not
add, remove or move a single opaque cell, and do not add a palette key.

1. Palette key `"3"` (the glass/empty fill): `#a9bcff` becomes `#c8d4ff`. Verified arithmetic, so
   the executor does not have to rediscover it: `relLum("#c8d4ff")` is 0.666 against
   `relLum("#4361ee")` 0.159, giving a contrast ratio of 3.42:1 — clear of the 3:1 floor with
   margin, while `#a9bcff` gives only 2.71:1 and cannot pass. `#c8d4ff` also stays 14.3:1 against
   the black outline (so the existing frozen-glyph contrast rule keeps passing) and is neither
   `DIALOGUE_BOX_COLOR` nor `DIALOGUE_TEXT_COLOR` (so `renderer.test.ts`'s achromatic dialogue guard
   keeps passing).
2. Rows 1 and 2 (zero-indexed): the nine interior cells of each currently hold `"2"`. Change them to
   `"3"`. The two end cells of each row stay `"1"`. Nothing else in the file changes — rows 0 and 12
   stay full outline plates, rows 3-9 keep their existing values, and rows 10 and 11 keep their
   nine `"2"` sand cells.

What that buys: the glyph's top-to-bottom luminance profile becomes dark plate, light glass for the
whole upper bulb, pinch, sand for the whole lower bulb, dark plate. That is the shape of a real
hourglass in any greyscale, so the state survives a desaturated or heavily compressed stream. Today
the profile is dark plate, SAND, light glass, pinch, sand, dark plate — the upper bulb's "empty"
read is buried under three rows of the same blue as the sand, which is why it glances as a bowtie.

Then add one new describe to `packages/pixel-office/src/sprites/bubbleSprites.test.ts`, placed after
the existing 1x silhouette describe and named for this gap. Reuse the file's own `relLum` and
`contrast` helpers and the same `waistRow` derivation the silhouette describe already computes
(minimum ink width strictly between the caps) — do not re-derive the geometry a second way. The
four behaviors above are the four cases. Derive the two fills the same way the existing sand test
does (the non-outline colours sorted by luminance, darker first), never by hard-coded hex, so the
test stays honest if the palette is retuned again. Exclude rows 0 and the last row from the band
counts — they are the plates, not bulb content.

Do not touch the four existing silhouette tests, the frozen-contrast describe, or any other glyph
asset.

One existing guard IS colour-sensitive rather than mask-sensitive, so "the mask is unchanged" does
not by itself cover it — check it explicitly rather than assuming. The frozen-contrast describe's
"legible on the MetroCity floor" rule picks the glyph's `mainFill` as its most-used non-outline
colour. Today that is `#4361ee` (51 cells against 18); after the repaint it flips to `#c8d4ff` (36
against `#4361ee`'s 33). The rule still passes, because the black outline term dominates its `max()`
against every floor-tile mean — but if it goes red, that flip is why, and the answer is to retune the
glass fill upward, never to weaken the rule.
